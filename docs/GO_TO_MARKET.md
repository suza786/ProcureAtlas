# Go-to-Market Strategy — ProcureAtlas AI

**Status:** Draft. Channel hypotheses require validation through customer discovery.
**Last updated:** 2026-09-30

This document defines the go-to-market strategy for ProcureAtlas AI: beachhead, channel hypotheses, content strategy, pricing entry path, expansion, and metrics to track.

---

## 1. Beachhead

### 1.1 Initial beachhead: Bangladesh IT / cybersecurity firms bidding domestically and to multilateral banks

**Why this beachhead:**

- **Underserved by global vendors** — GovWin, BGOV, BidNet, Beroe do not cover Bangladesh. TenderEasy / TenderTiger aggregate but offer no bid operations or intelligence layer.
- **Active bid volume** — Bangladesh CPTU publishes 30,000–50,000 tenders / year; World Bank and ADB finance significant Bangladesh procurement (transport, energy, IT, social sectors).
- **Real pain** — Bangladeshi IT / cybersecurity firms routinely miss World Bank / ADB eligibility windows; lack ISO 27001 visibility; struggle to assemble compliance matrices for international bids; lack a partner-finder for OEM relationships.
- **Founder-network access** — initial customer relationships exist in the Bangladesh IT / cybersecurity community.
- **Currency-aligned pricing** — Bangladesh tier pricing (৳2,990 / ৳9,900 / ৳29,900) is within reach of growing Bangladeshi firms.

### 1.2 Beachhead persona

- **Bid Manager + BD Lead** at a 50–300-person Bangladeshi IT / cybersecurity firm.
- Currently bids to: Bangladesh Computer Council, Bangladesh Bank, BRTA, NBR, World Bank-financed Bangladesh projects, ADB-financed Bangladesh projects.
- Pain: misses deadlines; loses bids on eligibility gaps discovered too late; cannot find OEM partners in time; cannot benchmark win rate.

### 1.3 Beachhead size

- ~200–400 target firms in Bangladesh IT / cybersecurity / consulting / engineering services sectors that bid to government and multilaterals.
- Realistic beachhead target: 20 paying customers in Year 1; 50 in Year 2.

## 2. Channel Hypotheses

These are hypotheses to be tested in customer discovery. None is committed.

### 2.1 LinkedIn outbound

- **Hypothesis:** targeted LinkedIn outreach to Bid Managers / BD Leads / Founders at Bangladesh IT / cybersecurity firms yields 5–10% meeting rate.
- **Tactic:** 50 personalized messages / week; offer a free 30-min "Eligibility audit" using the platform on a current tender of their choice.
- **Test:** 4-week sprint; measure meeting rate, demo-to-trial rate, trial-to-paid rate.

### 2.2 Tender consultant partnerships

- **Hypothesis:** independent tender consultants in Bangladesh (who run bids on behalf of multiple clients) become resellers / power users.
- **Tactic:** identify 10–15 active tender consultants via Bangladesh e-GP registered-bidder directories and LinkedIn; offer them free Professional tier in exchange for case studies.
- **Test:** 6-week partnership cycle; measure consultant-led customer acquisition.

### 2.3 BD team direct sales

- **Hypothesis:** a 2-person founder-led BD team (founder + 1 SDR) can close 5–8 deals / quarter in the beachhead through warm intros + in-person meetings in Dhaka.
- **Tactic:** weekly Dhaka in-person meetings; quarterly bid-management roundtable in Dhaka; demo using the prospect's own live tender of interest.
- **Test:** 90-day sprint; measure pipeline conversion rate.

### 2.4 Industry associations

- **Hypothesis:** Bangladesh IT industry associations (BASIS, ISPAB, BCC) are channels to reach the beachhead.
- **Tactic:** sponsor / speak at BASIS events; offer free workshops on "Eligibility for World Bank / ADB bids"; build email list.
- **Test:** 2 events / quarter; measure email-list growth and conversion.

### 2.5 Referral

- **Hypothesis:** won customers refer 0.3–0.5 new customers each in Year 1 via word-of-mouth in the tight Bangladesh IT community.
- **Tactic:** referral program — 1 month free for referrer + referred on any paid tier.
- **Test:** track referrals monthly.

## 3. Content Strategy

### 3.1 Pillars

1. **Market reports** — quarterly "Bangladesh Public Procurement Intelligence Report" (free, gated by email); covers tender volume, top buyers, sector trends, win-rate benchmarks.
2. **Bid-win teardowns** — anonymized case studies of won bids (with customer permission) showing how the platform was used; published monthly.
3. **Eligibility guides** — "How to win World Bank-financed IT bids in Bangladesh"; "ISO 27001 requirements for BCC cybersecurity tenders"; etc.
4. **Product updates** — monthly changelog; Loom videos for new features.
5. **LinkedIn thought leadership** — founder posts 2×/week on procurement intelligence, evidence-grounded bidding, and the Bangladesh market.

### 3.2 Distribution

- LinkedIn (primary) — founder + company page.
- Email newsletter (gated) — quarterly market report + monthly teardown.
- YouTube — Loom-style product walkthroughs.
- In-person — Dhaka bid-management roundtable (quarterly).

### 3.3 SEO

- Long-tail SEO on "Bangladesh tender [sector]" + "World Bank bid eligibility" + "ADB procurement Bangladesh".
- Programmatic pages per buyer (e.g., `/buyers/bangladesh-computer-council`) showing buyer profile + recent awards (Phase 3+ — Buyer Intelligence).

## 4. Pricing Entry Path

```
Free → Solo (BD ৳2,990) → Professional (BD ৳9,900) → Bid Team (BD ৳29,900)
Free → Intelligence ($49) → Pursuit Pro ($149) → Team ($399)
```

### 4.1 Activation path

1. Prospect signs up for Free (or 14-day trial of Professional / Pursuit Pro).
2. Onboarding wizard: set up Company Brain (≥ 5 evidence items) → save first pursuit → view 3 Tender Digital Twins.
3. In-app upgrade prompts at: 80% tenders tracked quota, 80% pursuits quota, evidence expiry approaching without Submission Readiness unlocked (Free tier).
4. Sales-assist: when a Free user reaches 80% quota, an SDR reaches out within 24 hours offering a Personalized demo.

### 4.2 Expansion path

- Solo → Professional: when a Solo user adds a second team member or approaches the 100-tender cap.
- Professional → Bid Team: when the team grows past 5 seats or 50 pursuits.
- Bid Team → Enterprise: when the customer requests SSO, SCIM, custom integrations, or multi-entity support.

## 5. Expansion

### 5.1 Singapore (Year 2)

- Second market: Singapore — well-structured data (GeBIZ + data.gov.sg), English-language, mature IT procurement market.
- Channel: direct sales + Singapore IT industry associations (SCS, SiTF).
- Pricing: International tier (USD).

### 5.2 Multilateral banks (Year 2)

- World Bank + ADB + UN as cross-cutting "markets" — relevant to any international bidder.
- Channel: content marketing (World Bank / ADB bid eligibility guides); partnerships with multilateral-bank-focused consultants.
- Pricing: International tier (USD).

### 5.3 EU TED (Year 3)

- TED is the largest open-data source in scope; significant volume.
- Channel: content marketing; partnerships with EU bid consultants; EU language localization (Phase 6).
- Pricing: International tier (USD) with EU-VAT-compliant billing.

### 5.4 US SAM.gov (Year 3)

- Mature US federal contracting market with established competitors (GovWin, BGOV).
- Channel: direct sales; differentiation via MCP + API + multi-market coverage.
- Pricing: International tier (USD).

### 5.5 Expansion markets (Year 4+)

- Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, India, Australia, UK, Canada, Middle East, Africa — per Phase 6 roadmap.

## 6. Metrics to Track

### 6.1 Acquisition

- Sign-ups / month (Free + trial).
- Demo meetings / month.
- Channel mix (LinkedIn / consultant / direct / association / referral).
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) per channel.

### 6.2 Activation

- Activation rate: % of sign-ups who complete Company Brain setup (≥ 5 evidence items) + save first pursuit within 7 days.
- Time-to-first-value: time from sign-up to first saved pursuit.

### 6.3 Retention

- Weekly Active Users / Monthly Active Users (WAU/MAU) ≥ 0.4 target.
- Monthly logo retention ≥ 95% for paid.
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR) ≥ 110% by end of Year 2.

### 6.4 Paid conversion

- Free → paid conversion rate ≥ 5% within 90 days of sign-up.
- Trial → paid conversion rate ≥ 25%.
- Average contract value (ACV) by tier.

### 6.5 Advocacy

- Net Promoter Score (NPS) ≥ 40 within 6 months of paid launch.
- Referral rate (referrals per customer per year) ≥ 0.3.

### 6.6 Value

- Self-reported win rate uplift: ≥ 10 percentage points for paid tenants vs. their pre-ProcureAtlas baseline.
- Time from tender publication to pursuit creation: ≤ 3 days (vs. industry baseline 7–14 days).
- Compliance matrix completion rate at submission: ≥ 90% of mandatory requirements.

### 6.7 Unit economics

- Gross margin ≥ 75% (SaaS benchmark).
- LTV / CAC ≥ 3.
- Payback period ≤ 12 months.

## 7. Risks + Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Bangladesh beachhead too small | Expand to Singapore + multilateral banks in Year 2 |
| Direct sales motion too slow | Layer in self-serve + content marketing early |
| Competitor enters Bangladesh | Lock in beachhead customers with annual prepay + integrations |
| Free tier cannibalizes paid | Tight Free tier quotas; clear upgrade prompts |
| Pricing set wrong | Customer discovery interviews before launch (see `PRICING.md` §9) |

## 9. Open Questions

- Should we offer a 30-day money-back guarantee on annual prepay? (Leaning: yes, for first-year customers only.)
- Should we have a separate "Tender Consultant" tier with multi-tenant-from-one-account features? (Leaning: yes, Phase 3.)
- Should we offer implementation services (paid onboarding) for Enterprise? (Leaning: yes, partner with a Bangladesh-based bid consultancy.)
- Should we open-source any part of the platform (e.g., the OCDS-inspired canonical model library) for credibility? (Leaning: evaluate at Phase 5.)

These questions are resolved in the Phase 3 GTM plan, informed by Phase 2 design-partner feedback.
